# bonsai-fold: Research Plan

## 1. Motivation and central question

PrismML's Bonsai 27B moves the entire Qwen3.6-27B network into binary ({-1,+1}) or ternary ({-1,0,+1}) weights with g128 FP16 scales, end to end, retaining 89.5% (1-bit) and 94.6% (ternary) of the full-precision benchmark average. Their whitepaper also shows (Table 6) that these models tolerate 4-bit KV-cache quantization with 12x to 95x less output divergence than the FP16 baseline: the conversion demonstrably reshaped the model's robustness to value-level noise.

Depth pruning research (ShortGPT, LLM-Streamline, SLEB, and successors) shows full-precision LLMs carry substantial inter-layer redundancy: entire blocks can be removed with modest damage. Nobody has measured whether that structural slack survives an end-to-end move to 1 to 1.71 bits per weight. Two live hypotheses point in opposite directions, and either outcome is a finding:

- **Consumption:** precision compression and depth compression draw on the same redundancy budget; the 1-bit model has little or no structural slack left.
- **Robustness transfer:** the same shaping that makes these models unusually tolerant of KV discretization also makes them tolerant of structural perturbation; slack remains and can be harvested training-free.

Practical payoff if slack exists: a 27B-class model already at 3.9 GB gets smaller and proportionally faster (decode is memory-bandwidth-bound, so bytes removed convert directly to latency), with zero training and zero format changes. Practical payoff if it does not: first quantitative evidence that depth and precision compression are non-orthogonal at the extreme end.

## 2. Hypotheses

- **H1:** The 1-bit model retains measurable depth redundancy: at least one block is removable with near-zero output KL.
- **H2:** Structural slack shrinks with precision aggressiveness: the damage-per-dropped-block slope is steeper for 1-bit than for ternary.
- **H3 (competing with H2):** Noise-shaped models tolerate structural perturbation better than their retention gap suggests, mirroring the KV-tolerance result.
- **H4:** Format-native merging beats dropping at matched depth reduction, because disagreement-abstention preserves shared computation.
- **H5:** Droppability differs systematically by block type (full attention vs linear attention) and depth position; the hybrid pattern must be respected.

## 3. Comparison design

Two arms only: **1-bit Bonsai 27B (primary)** and **Ternary Bonsai 27B (comparison and fallback)**. No FP16, FP8, or conventional-quant arms; published whitepaper numbers serve as fixed reference points only. All damage metrics are within-format: folded 1-bit vs unfolded 1-bit, folded ternary vs unfolded ternary. Every experiment completes on the 1-bit model before its ternary counterpart is considered. Ternary becomes primary only if Gate A fires.

## 4. Metrics and screening protocol

**KL screen (primary, cheap).** Build two frozen probe sets once: (a) on-policy: 100 MATH-500 prompts, completions generated by the unfolded 1-bit model in thinking mode, token sequences frozen; (b) off-policy: 100 fixed chunks (roughly 2-4K tokens each) of a long-context corpus. For each folded candidate, teacher-force the frozen tokens through both folded and unfolded models and report mean per-token forward KL (nats, full vocabulary). This mirrors the whitepaper's own tolerance methodology, applied to structural perturbation. Both models fit in memory simultaneously.

**Anchor calibration.** Provisional gate thresholds below are guesses. Early in Phase 2, run mini-bench on 3 anchor configs spanning low, medium, and high KL, fit the KL-to-score-drop relationship, then recalibrate all thresholds and record the mapping in the lab notebook. Intuition anchor: the whitepaper's 4-bit KV perturbation induced ~0.0009 nats on-policy on the 1-bit model, so KL in that range is effectively free.

**Delta PPL.** Secondary sanity metric on a frozen corpus. Absolute perplexity of low-bit models can be odd; only within-format deltas are meaningful.

**Mini-bench.** GSM8K-100, MATH500-100, IFEval subset (~100), MMLU-Redux-200. Thinking mode, sampling per the facts doc (temp 0.7, top-p 0.95, top-k 20), rule-based scoring with a thinking-block parser. At ~26 tok/s and thinking budgets this is hours per config: reserve for KL-screened survivors, run overnight, checkpoint per item.

**Efficiency accounting per config:** deployed GB removed, params removed, expected and measured decode tok/s, KV-cache bytes per token removed (nonzero only when full-attention blocks go).

## 5. Phases

### Phase 0: Bootstrap and model census (1-2 days)

Environment (uv, mlx, mlx-lm, huggingface_hub, datasets), resolve the day-1 unknowns in the facts doc, download the 1-bit 27B MLX pack, produce a per-block inventory: block index, attention type, tensor names and shapes, quantization bits and group size, param count. Identify the full-attention indices and interleave pattern. Verify the 1-bit affine packing empirically (paper: w = s_mlx * q + b_mlx with q in {0,1}, s_mlx = 2*s_g, b_mlx = -s_g). Decide whether vision-tower tensors can be stripped for text-only work (~0.9 GB saved). Smoke-test generation, record tok/s and peak memory. Then stop and propose Phase 1 specifics.

### Phase 1: Redundancy mapping (2-4 days, inference only)

Hook the residual stream at block boundaries on a fixed calibration set (50-100 diverse sequences, mixed reasoning and prose). Compute per-block influence: BI_i = 1 - E[cos(h_in, h_out)] (ShortGPT-style), plus an angular-distance variant, reported separately for full-attention and linear-attention blocks. Also compute pairwise similarity between candidate adjacent same-type blocks (needed for Phase 3 pair selection). Deliverable: the redundancy map of a 1-bit end-to-end model, which to our knowledge does not exist in the literature. Repeat on ternary afterwards for the H2/H3 comparison (cheap, same harness).

**Gate A:** if no single-block removal on the 1-bit model achieves on-policy KL below ~0.05 nats (provisional), conclude the 1-bit model has near-zero structural slack, document it as a finding, promote ternary to primary, and keep the 1-bit result as the comparison point.

### Phase 2: Drop-based folding (1-2 weeks)

Remove k blocks selected by the Phase 1 map, respecting the hybrid pattern. Mechanics: delete modules from the layer list, update depth and any per-layer-type config metadata; surviving weights untouched. Sweep k in {1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16} with greedy and clustered selection; KL-screen everything; mini-bench the Pareto set. Ablations for H5: matched-k drops of only-linear vs including-full-attention blocks; early vs mid vs late depth. Efficiency check: confirm decode tok/s rises roughly with bytes removed.

**Gate B (defines "works"):** at least one config removes 4 or more blocks with a mini-bench average drop of at most ~1.5 points (provisional). If nothing passes, proceed to Phase 3 anyway (merging may succeed where dropping fails); if Phase 3 also fails on 1-bit, fall back to ternary per the standing rule.

### Phase 3: Format-native merging (1-2 weeks)

Merging adjacent near-identity blocks approximates x + f_A(x) + f_B(x + f_A(x)) with x + f_merged(x); this is principled exactly in the high-similarity regime Phase 1 identifies. Only same-type adjacent (or near-adjacent) pairs are merged. Three operators, all closed-form and training-free, applied tensor-by-tensor on aligned g128 groups:

1. **Sign-election merge (binary pair to binary block).** W_avg = (s_A*t_A + s_B*t_B)/2; new scale per group = mean(|W_avg|) over the group (absmean); new sign = sign(W_avg), ties broken toward t_A. Output stays 1-bit.
2. **Promotion merge (binary pair to ternary block).** t' = t_A where t_A == t_B, else 0; s' = (s_A + s_B)/2 per group. Disagreement becomes abstention, which binary cannot represent and ternary can, so the merged block runs on the existing 2-bit path. This yields a heterogeneous-precision stack (mostly 1-bit blocks, a few ternary merged blocks), representable in MLX since bits are per-layer. Two blocks at 1.125 bpw become one at ~2 bpw deployed: net weight savings plus one block's worth of compute and latency.
3. **Ternary election merge (ternary pair to ternary block, comparison arm).** Same election rule natively in trit space; projection variant: absmean re-ternarization of W_avg (BitNet b1.58-style round-and-clip).

Non-matrix parameters (RMSNorm gains, any FP16 tail) are FP-averaged. Linear-attention blocks may carry state or gating parameters where averaging is dubious; the Phase 0 census decides, and any suspicious parameter class excludes those pairs from merging until understood. Evaluate merges vs drops at matched block-count reduction (H4), KL screen then mini-bench.

### Phase 4: DEFERRED. Healing and extensions (out of scope, for future reference only)

Recorded so context is not lost; nothing here is to be implemented now.

- **LoRA healing:** FP16 adapters (rank 8-32) on the frozen folded low-bit base, QLoRA-style, adapters kept unmerged at serving so the base stays exactly binary or ternary. Data via self-distillation from the unfolded model of the same format (teacher and student fit together in 64 GB). Precedent: layer-prune-then-heal on FP models (Gromov et al.); unknown whether healing works on an end-to-end low-bit base.
- **Q4_K_XL control arm:** only if a reviewer demands a conventional-quant pruning curve.
- **DSpark drafter retap:** the shipped drafter taps five evenly spaced target layers, so folding breaks it. Until retapped, all folded-model runs disable speculative decoding.
- **Sub-bit packing of survivors:** entropy-coding measured trit distributions, scale quantization.

## 6. Deliverables

Redundancy maps (1-bit, ternary), damage curves per operator and per format, the heterogeneous-stack result, a released folded pack if Gate B passes, and a short paper or technical report. Working title: "Is There Depth Left to Fold? Structural Redundancy in End-to-End 1-Bit LLMs." All three headline outcomes (slack harvested; slack gone, consumption confirmed; merging works where dropping fails) are publishable.

## 7. Risks

- PrismML's MLX inference path for the hybrid architecture may live in their own code rather than stock mlx-lm; Phase 0 resolves this first.
- Thinking-mode evals are slow locally; mitigated by the KL-first ladder and overnight checkpointed runs.
- Linear-attention block internals may complicate merging; drops are unaffected.
- Heterogeneous stacks assume per-layer bit-width loading works in the MLX path; verify with a toy merge before building the full harness.
